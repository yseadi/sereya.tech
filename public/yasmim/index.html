Point of view

The future is something you can rehearse.

Most organisations meet the future too late, as a trend report or a crisis. I help teams get there earlier, while there is still time to choose.

  1. 01

    Signals before trends

    By the time something is a trend, the window to act is closing. I look earlier, at odd data points and small changes in behaviour or policy.

  2. 02

    Futures, plural

    Forecasts fail when they pretend there is only one future. Scenarios let teams test their decisions against several.

  3. 03

    People at the centre

    Technology matters through what it changes in people's lives: who gains, who is left out, and why.

  4. 04

    Rigour over hype

    Imagination needs a method, and every insight should trace back to evidence.

How I see

Everything I know about the future, I learned by paying attention.

I believe that the way I perceive the world, design strategies, and anticipate the future is a direct result of all the layered experiences that shape who I am. Raised in Brasil with deep Lubnani roots, I grew up navigating cultural intersections. Living in France for eight months further expanded this global lens through daily interactions with people from all over the world—from North Africa and the Levant to across Europe and the US—deepening my empathy and sensitivity to diverse social and geopolitical landscapes.

This background breathes through my daily rituals and artistic inspirations. Artists like Saint Levant and Beyoncé are central to how I explore ancestry: listening to Saint Levant’s trilingual album Deira sparked my desire to connect through multiple languages and dialects, while Beyoncé’s visionary work—especially in The Lion King: The Gift—teaches me about discipline, detail, and bringing ancestral roots into contemporary spaces.

My approach to reality is also guided by synaesthesia, allowing me to process sounds, rhythms, and textures in a distinctly sensory way, paired with an endless curiosity that spans from curating Spotify playlists to practicing strategic focus on the Brasilian Jiu-Jitsu mat and deconstructing dystopian narratives like The Handmaid’s Tale. Ultimately, this singular blend of personal perspectives and radical curiosity forms the bedrock of my professional practice, enabling me to translate complex signals into human-centered, high-impact digital strategies.

GIZtechDetector

Technology, measured by what it does for people. A platform co-developed by Envisioning and GIZ, Germany's development agency, to analyse the impact of emerging technologies on sustainable development.

Technology radar · Impact portal · Editorial programme Visit the case ↗
The approach

A radar built around sustainable development

techDetector connects new technologies to the challenges of sustainable development. It used a methodology Envisioning and GIZ created together, which looked at technology in a way international rankings did not.

Around the radar, an editorial line and a publishing schedule added case studies, interviews, future scenarios and even science fiction, all written with sustainability at the centre.

From 2020 to 2024 the platform added workshops and updates on futures literacy and technology foresight. They paid particular attention to effects on sustainability and gender equality, in line with the SDGs.

Timeline

2019 – 2024Launch, then five years of updates and workshops

Partners

Reach

Recognition

What we did

Five layers of one platform

01

Technology research

A custom methodology showing how emerging technologies depend on each other, with Technology Readiness Levels and historical data for each one.

02

Custom metrics

Gender and sustainability impact metrics co-designed with GIZ and reviewed with its teams and external experts.

03

Data architecture

Technologies classified into domains, applications and methods, a structure that organised the whole website.

04

Workshops

Sessions with stakeholders that spread the research, fed the metrics and opened new collaborations for GIZ.

05

Editorial & launch

Case studies, interviews and scenarios, plus launches in Berlin and online, newsletters and talks at conferences.

The question behind the platform

Who gains and who is left out when a new technology arrives?

How it worked

From signal to decision

  1. Step 01

    Map

    Identify emerging technologies and classify them into domains, applications and methods, each with its readiness level and history.

  2. Step 02

    Assess

    Score each technology on gender and sustainability impact, using metrics co-designed with GIZ and aligned with the SDGs.

  3. Step 03

    Contextualise

    Publish case studies, interviews, scenarios and fiction that connect each technology to real-world use.

  4. Step 04

    Engage

    Run workshops with GIZ teams, experts and partners that refine the data and turn insight into collaboration.

AchamosSua Criança

Technology built to bring children home. An open-source family reunification platform created during the 2024 floods in Rio Grande do Sul, southern Brasil.

Social technology · Open source · LGPD @achamossuacrianca ↗
The approach

Urgency, with responsibility

In May 2024, floods displaced hundreds of thousands of people in Rio Grande do Sul, and many children reached shelters without their parents.

We co-created an open-source platform to connect unaccompanied minors with their families. It had to go live fast, while the emergency was still happening.

Because the platform handled sensitive personal data of unaccompanied minors, legal compliance came first. We worked with legal advisors, the Guardianship Council and the Public Prosecutor's Office so that every record was protected under Brasil's General Data Protection Law (LGPD), shared with care and used only for family reunification.

Timeline

May 2024Built and deployed during the emergency

Context

Rio Grande do Sul floodsSouthern Brasil

Framework

Coverage

What we did

Four commitments

01

Reunification platform

An open-source tool to match unaccompanied children in shelters with the families searching for them.

02

Data protection

Sensitive data of minors handled under the LGPD, shared responsibly and used only for reunification.

03

Institutional coordination

Close work with the Guardianship Council, the Public Prosecutor's Office and legal advisors.

04

Public outreach

An Instagram presence that helped families find information quickly and reached national media.

What guided every decision

Move fast for the families. Move carefully for the children.
